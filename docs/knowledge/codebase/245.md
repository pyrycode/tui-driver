# #245 — Retire the `agents` modal classifier arm

[Issue](https://github.com/pyrycode/tui-driver/issues/245) · Reverses [#182](182.md)'s "keep dormant" decision · Blocks [#244](https://github.com/pyrycode/tui-driver/issues/244) · Code: `pkg/tuidriver/modal.go`, `pkg/tuidriver/agents.go`, `pkg/tuidriver/modal_test.go`, `pkg/tuidriver/anchor_forgery_test.go`

## Summary

#182 kept the `agents` classifier arm dormant on a no-observed-cost basis (retain-not-retire, four doc-only annotations, zero executable change). This ticket supplies the observed cost: the #227 `cmd/corpus-replay` harness showed a healthy production recording (`20260703T204559Z-…-ok.cast`) classifying `agents` **six times** on plain transcript content. Since claude 2.1.199 removed the `/agents` wizard, every one of those fires is false by definition on the pinned claude — the two pillars #182 stood on (no observed cost; a ≤2.1.158 build sits inside the lock's tolerated-drift envelope) are both gone: `claude-version.lock` pins `version=2.1.199` and tolerates drift **above** that version, not below it, so a pre-2.1.199 build was never actually in the envelope.

**Decision: retire, not version-gate.** A real version gate would mean threading a version/`enablePre2199Modals` value through `DetectModalClass` → `detectModalClassWithGrid` and every caller (`events.go`'s `classify`, `HasUnknownDialog`, `isSlashPicker`, `ParseModalContent`/`AnswerModal`) — an exported-signature break with edit fan-out to preserve a path with zero current consumers. Retire deletes ~8 lines and adds zero surface.

**What changed (`pkg/tuidriver/modal.go`):** the `case … → ModalClassAgents` arm is deleted from `detectModalClassWithGrid`, along with its three now-orphaned `anchorAgents*` vars and the agents anchor-doc entry. `ModalClassAgents` the constant is **retained** — its comment is rewritten to record the #245 retirement rationale and point at the retained artifacts, and is now the single source of truth (the `ModalClass` type-doc bullet just points at it). Every other arm's predicate and switch order is byte-identical.

**What stayed:** `ParseAgentList`/`AgentList` (parser body untouched, doc comment gets a one-line pointer refresh in `agents.go`), `agents-snapshot.bin`, `TestParseAgentListRealFixture`, `TestParseAgentListEmpty`, and `cmd/spike-multiselect/main.go`'s `case tuidriver.ModalClassAgents:` dispatch (still compiles, now unreachable via `DetectModalClass` but harmless). `claude-version.lock` and the e2e `agents` snapshot case were not touched or re-introduced.

**Fall-through verified, not assumed.** The two candidate misclassifications for a de-armed agents render were checked and closed: the "Enter to select" footer doesn't reach `ask-user-question` because the only `❯` row (`❯ /agents`) isn't a numbered/checkbox option row (`selectionOptionRe` doesn't match it); the `❯ /agents` row doesn't reach `slash-picker` either because `pickerRowStartRe` requires the row to start with `/`, not `❯`. Ground-truth checked with the arm physically removed: `agents-snapshot.bin` → `ModalClassUnknown`, `HasUnknownDialog` → `false`. Every other committed fixture (`mcp`, `picker`, `permission`, `trust-folder`, `ask-user-question-screen`) still classifies to its own class unchanged.

## Patterns established

- **Reversing a prior "keep dormant" decision needs the original decision's basis to have actually changed, not just "we could delete this now."** #182's dormant call rested on two named pillars (no observed cost + tolerated-drift envelope coverage). #245 didn't re-litigate the decision from scratch — it showed both pillars were now false (one by new evidence, #227's corpus harness; one by re-reading the lock policy correctly — drift is tolerated *above* the pin, not below). When revisiting a prior retain/dormant call, name the specific pillars and show which one moved.
- **Single-source-of-truth comment placement survives a structural removal.** #182 established "one primary rationale comment on the exported symbol (`ModalClassAgents`), one-line pointers elsewhere." #245 kept that shape even though the thing being documented flipped from "why this is dormant" to "why this arm is gone" — the type-doc bullet and the anchor-doc entry still just point at the const's comment rather than re-stating the rationale.
- **A classifier-arm removal's safety case is the fall-through analysis, not the diff.** Deleting a `case` doesn't remove the bytes it used to match — it changes which *other* arm (or `Unknown`) they now hit. The load-bearing verification here was checking the two structurally-plausible fall-through targets (`ask-user-question`, `slash-picker`) against their actual regexes and confirming ground truth (`agents-snapshot.bin` → `Unknown`), not reasoning from the diff alone. Reusable checklist for any future arm retirement in `detectModalClassWithGrid`.

## Lessons learned

- **`security-sensitive` gated on the surface touched even though the removed arm was never a grant surface.** Per #182's precedent, the label is the gate regardless of the chosen path's blast radius. The full adversarial walk (verdict PASS) confirmed the one real question — can removing this arm reroute a permission/trust render into a wrong class — is "no," and additionally confirmed the *new*-misclassification risk (agents falling through to ask-user or slash-picker) is closed. One honest residual recorded, not defended: a hypothetical *populated* pre-2.1.199 agents modal (numbered option rows) could fall through to `ask-user-question`, but it's unreachable on the pinned claude, no current consumer runs pre-2.1.199, and no fixture exists to capture it (the upstream feature is gone).
- **The lock's "tolerated drift" direction is easy to misread once and then carry forward.** #182 treated "≤2.1.158 is inside the tolerated-drift envelope" as settled. Re-reading `claude-version.lock` directly for #245 showed the envelope is defined as drift *above* `version=2.1.199`, not below — the earlier framing had the direction backwards. Worth a direct re-read of the lock file's actual wording rather than trusting a prior ticket's paraphrase, when a decision hinges on it.
