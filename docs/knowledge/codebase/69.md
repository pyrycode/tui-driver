# #69 — spike-cancel: PTY-quiescence in both `waitReappeared` and `runRecovery`

[Issue](https://github.com/pyrycode/tui-driver/issues/69) · [Spec](../../specs/architecture/69-spike-cancel-pty-quiescence.md) · Code: `cmd/spike-cancel/`, `cmd/e2e-runner/`

## Summary

`spike-cancel` wedged every `make e2e` run against `claude 2.1.148` at Probe 1 (`kindThinking`, the 1000-word monad essay) with `watchdog: stuck after cancel for 30s`. Total wall time ~32.9 s — Probe 1 setup + the 30 s `cancelRecoveryLimit` deadline inside `waitReappeared` (`cmd/spike-cancel/main.go:513-568` pre-fix). The wedge is mechanically identical to [#73 / PR #74](73.md) (`spike-multi-turn` `runTurn`) and [#70](70.md) (`spike-permission` `runAutoRespond`): claude paints a `✻` spinner glyph during processing; post-cancel claude emits ~1.4 KB of input-box-restore + title-bar updates and falls silent — 1577 bytes of churn since the last `✻` paint at watchdog-trip time, well under the 4096-byte rolling-buffer cap (`pkg/tuidriver/buffer.go:8-13`). The stale `✻` paint stays in the buffer; `tuidriver.IsIdle`'s spinner-absent half (`pkg/tuidriver/state.go:38-44`) stays false forever; the predicate wedges. Subtle history twist: `waitReappeared` *already* had `rb.QuietFor() >= ptyQuietWindow` in its predicate from #11 — but the conjunction was `tuidriver.IsIdle(rb.Snapshot()) ∧ QuietFor()`, so the IsIdle wrapper was still wedging the conjunction even though the quiescence half was satisfied trivially.

The second predicate site in this file (`runRecovery` at `cmd/spike-cancel/main.go:593-688` pre-fix) carried the **verbatim pre-#74** `spike-multi-turn` predicate — `gotEndTurn ∧ tuidriver.IsIdle(rb) stable for idleStableWindow = 250ms`. Probes 3, 4, 5 are all `kindRecovery` and run this predicate. The wedge never surfaced in Step 1's diagnosis because Probe 1's 30 s fire is earlier in the linear flow — but the predicate shape is the one PR #74 already proved wedges, and the byte-count substrate is identical. Bundled into the same fix to avoid the unmasking-on-next-run trap that [#70's lesson set](70.md) flagged.

Fix is a one-binary swap of both predicates to the canonical PTY-quiescence shape (`bytes.Contains(StripANSI(rb.Snapshot()), tuidriver.IdleGlyph) ∧ rb.QuietFor() ≥ ptyQuietWindow`), dropping the `tuidriver.IsIdle` wrapper at both sites. `idleStableWindow = 250 * time.Millisecond` is removed (no other consumers in this file); the `idleSince` local in `runRecovery` is removed (the temporal accumulator now lives inside `QuietFor`). `ptyQuietWindow = 1500 * time.Millisecond` is unchanged — same empirical window calibrated in #11, reused at four sites now. The `❯-disappeared` observer goroutine in `runRecovery` (`cmd/spike-cancel/main.go:606-626`) still calls `tuidriver.IsIdle` unchanged — it's bounded by `disappearedWindow = 500ms` and emits best-effort logging only, can't wedge. No exported package symbols change.

A second commit bumps `spike-cancel`'s per-check timeout in `cmd/e2e-runner/main.go` from the default 60 s to **120 s** because post-fix wall time is ~60–70 s on `claude 2.1.148`. Probe 4 re-runs the 1000-word monad essay as a real recovery turn and that's ~30–40 s of model time on its own; the default 60 s cut runs short at exactly 60002 ms with no probe hitting its internal watchdog. The spec's § Step 2 estimate of 18–25 s for the 5-probe shape undercounted Probe 4's full essay generation by 2–3×; the timeout bump is structural, not a workaround for a flaky binary.

Verified per AC: `./bin/spike-cancel -trust-folder=accept` on `claude 2.1.148` exits 0 with three `SUCCESS:` lines (Probes 3/4/5 are `kindRecovery` and emit success markers); total wall time ~63 s; no probe hits its internal watchdog. `make e2e` reports `spike-cancel: pass`; `claude-version-lock` still passes; no adjacent regression.

## Patterns established

- **PTY-quiescence is now a four-consumer pattern across three binaries.** Post-cancel readiness ([#11](11.md), `waitReappeared`), multi-turn turn-complete ([#73](73.md), `runTurn`), post-approve readiness ([#70](70.md), `runAutoRespond`), and post-cancel-recovery readiness (this ticket, `runRecovery`). Four independent state transitions — cancel-then-redraw, end_turn-then-next-turn, approve-then-respond, end_turn-after-recovery — converged on `desired-event-observed ∧ bytes.Contains(StripANSI(snap), tuidriver.IdleGlyph) ∧ rb.QuietFor() ≥ 1500 ms`. The library extraction (#58–#62) now has decisive integration pressure; the public API needs a `ReadyForInput`-shaped predicate distinct from `IsIdle` (which keeps the spinner-absent half for callers that want it). See [system overview § Key signals](../architecture/system-overview.md).
- **`tuidriver.IsIdle` is the wedging primitive in three of those four sites — drop the wrapper, compose narrower predicates directly.** All four sites now call `bytes.Contains(tuidriver.StripANSI(rb.Snapshot()), tuidriver.IdleGlyph)` rather than `tuidriver.IsIdle(rb.Snapshot())`. The pattern: `StripANSI` and `IdleGlyph` are exported precisely so consumers can compose narrower predicates than `IsIdle`; reach for the narrower composition when the spinner-absent clause is the wedging clause. The post-extraction API should likely surface "ReadyForInput" (❯ present + quiescence) as the canonical readiness signal and demote `IsIdle` to "Idle (no processing visible)" — they answer different questions, and four consumers have now consistently wanted the former.
- **When two predicate sites share a file, mechanism, and fix shape, fix them in one ticket.** The architect's spec deliberately bundled `waitReappeared` (empirically observed wedge) and `runRecovery` (structural-identity wedge by transfer from PR #74) rather than routing `runRecovery` through a separate ticket. Splitting would have burned a second pipeline cycle on structurally-identical work in the same file. The discipline: the "Evidence-Based Fix Selection" principle is satisfied when the evidence is the **union** of (a) the direct observation for one site and (b) the proven sibling for the other site that uses the same predicate shape on the same buffer substrate. If both sites are in the same file and the fix is the same closure rewrite twice, bundling is correct. If the second site lives in a different binary or needs a different empirical constant, split.
- **Per-check timeouts in the e2e runner are part of the structural contract for byte-stream-coupled checks.** `cmd/e2e-runner/main.go` defaults to 60 s per check, but `spike-cancel` (now 120 s) and `snapshot-drift` (180 s) both have hardcoded overrides. The pattern is: if a check's expected wall time on the current `claude` version is within 50% of the default 60 s, bump it deliberately in the runner rather than relying on operator-side `-timeout` overrides. The runner's default is for short checks; long-running checks (those that exercise real model generation, like Probe 4's essay) should declare their needs structurally.

## Lessons learned

- **A historical "fix" can leave a half-broken predicate behind.** `waitReappeared`'s predicate was originally written in [#11](11.md) as `tuidriver.IsIdle ∧ rb.QuietFor() ≥ ptyQuietWindow` — the quiescence half was added because the spec's first-cut predicate (`hasSpinnerGlyph false AND IsIdle true`) was empirically broken. But the IsIdle wrapper *stayed in the conjunction*. On `claude 2.1.144` this didn't matter (the post-cancel byte volume was high enough to roll the glyph out; both halves flipped true together). On `claude 2.1.148` the byte volume shrank and IsIdle stays false forever — the quiescence half is satisfied trivially but the conjunction wedges. Lesson: when adding a "stronger" half to a conjunction as a fix, ask whether the original half is now redundant or actively unsafe. The #11 fix was "add quiescence to make the predicate work" but should have been "replace the IsIdle wrapper with quiescence." The half-step left the file primed for the 2.1.148 regression.
- **Wall-time decomposition is the right diagnostic primitive for fail-fast e2e checks.** [#71's lesson set](71.md) framed it generally; this ticket validates it again. The 32.9 s pre-fix wall time decomposed cleanly: ~2.9 s Probe 1 setup + 30 s `cancelRecoveryLimit` watchdog. The decomposition fingered the watchdog as the binding constraint and `waitReappeared` as the wedge site before any code was read. Conversely, the post-fix 60-second-runner-cut produced 60002 ms with no probe hitting an internal watchdog — that decomposition fingered the runner-side cut as the binding constraint and motivated the timeout bump rather than further spike-side investigation. Future diagnoses should always decompose observed wall times against the sum of named timeouts; if the runtime can be cleanly accounted for by `setup + named timeout`, the named constant is the binding constraint and the fix lives there.
- **The architect's "diagnose first, then fix" branching was load-bearing — but the diagnosis closed quickly.** The original spec for this ticket routed two branches: Branch A (the strong-prior `runRecovery` swap mirroring PR #74) and Branch B (`waitReappeared` is wedging too, requiring a re-spec). The developer's Step 1 evidence landed in Branch B (Probe 1 wedge in `waitReappeared`, not `runRecovery`). The architect re-spec then prescribed both swaps in one ticket because the byte-count substrate transferred cleanly. Without the branching, a naive copy of PR #74's diff would have fixed `runRecovery` and shipped a still-broken binary (Probe 1 still wedges). The branching cost was small (Step 1 took two reproducible runs) and the payoff was large (correct fix scope in one ticket). Lesson: when pattern-matching against a recent successful fix is the strong prior, branch the spec rather than copy-paste; the cost of one diagnostic step is much lower than a follow-up rework cycle.
- **The version-lock short-circuit hid two structurally-identical wedges in adjacent files (not just one).** [#70's lesson](70.md) flagged that `claude-version-lock`'s short-circuit (now removed by [#64](64.md)) masked the `runAutoRespond` wedge for ~2 months. This ticket's diagnosis surfaces that the **same** short-circuit also masked `waitReappeared`'s wedge — same `claude 2.1.148` regression, same buffer-residue mechanism, same broken consumer line. Both wedges had likely been live since `claude 2.1.148` shipped (the byte budget shrank then), and both were invisible until #64 landed. Cumulative cost across the diagnose-and-fix batch (#69–#72): four wedges hidden under one short-circuit. Confirms [#70's actionable defence](70.md): when a long-standing CI check is short-circuited under "the environment doesn't match the lock," assume **multiple** checks behind the short-circuit are deterministically broken, not just one — and remove the short-circuit eagerly. The post-hoc cost (four diagnose-and-fix tickets) is much higher than the would-have-been cost (catching one regression at the time it landed and fixing it before it dominoed).
- **A "documented finding" doesn't propagate to the same binary's *other* call sites either.** [#71's lesson](71.md) and [#70's lesson](70.md) flagged that conventions don't retroactively propagate across spike binaries — but this ticket reveals an even narrower failure mode: the `waitReappeared` PTY-quiescence finding from [#11](11.md) was documented in the **same binary's** README (`cmd/spike-cancel/README.md` finding 1) and yet `runRecovery` (also in `cmd/spike-cancel/main.go`) kept the broken predicate shape. The `// copied from spike-multi-turn` attribution chain produces this: `runRecovery` was copied from spike-multi-turn's `runTurn` *before* #11's quiescence pattern was understood in `waitReappeared`, and nobody swept the file post-#11 to harmonise. Lesson: when a finding lands inside a binary's README, the developer should sweep the **rest of the same binary** for predicate-shapes that the finding makes obsolete. The cross-binary sweep is the expensive one ([#70](70.md)'s actionable defence); the intra-binary sweep is cheap and was skipped here.
